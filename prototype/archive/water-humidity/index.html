Model notes

The four fields

Water is a depth per cell. Only water above the retention threshold is mobile. It moves down the depression-filled heightmap, and a second, independent flux carries it up the grouping-demand gradient so neighbouring water joins existing channels.

Grouping demand is a bounded 0–1 field. It is emitted where water is both fast and deep (turnover speed times depth), spreads by diffusion and decays over time. A still sea and a thin film emit nothing; a river emits a lot. Water below the film threshold also resists infiltration when it is moving, which lets a river's nose advance while a stagnant puddle disappears.

Humidity diffuses and drifts with the prevailing wind. It spawns from surface water at a rate suppressed by grouping demand (water / (1 + hold × G)), so a river keeps its water instead of evaporating it. It despawns as rain, gated by the square of grouping demand above a threshold.

Rain is a transfer from air to land. Because the gate is on grouping demand, and sea cells never build it, no rain falls over the sea — the sea feeds the air, the air rains on rivers, and rivers discharge back into the sea.

Why the model is shaped this way

A single potential height − G turns a wet channel into a potential well: dry cells ahead have lower demand, so the river's nose faces an uphill barrier and stops. Splitting downhill flow and up-demand flow into two independent non-negative fluxes keeps the nose advancing while still clustering water sideways.

Depression filling gives every cell a downhill path to the sea; without it, noise pits trap all water and rain floods the map. A depth-gated, capped surface term lets deep lakes level out of those flats without letting a thin sheet spread over hills.

Rivers need seeds. A global drizzle would create exactly the thin layer this model avoids, so a few mountain springs start the channels; the grouping/rain feedback then selects and reinforces the strongest ones.

Things to try

Switch rain to Everywhere — the whole map floods with a slow-moving sheet. Turn off the grouping field — rain stops and channels weaken. Set sea evaporation to zero — the inland rivers lose their moisture supply. Try the valleys terrain for a gentler cross-slope, islands for separate basins and basin for a closed lake. The Depression-fill weight slider dials the priority-flood drainage surface from fully filled to raw terrain.
