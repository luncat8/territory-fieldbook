## 1. Technical Foundation

The prototype uses **plain JavaScript with minimal HTML and CSS**, and renders with the **Canvas 2D API**. Performance follows a data-oriented approach: **typed arrays** and **struct-of-arrays** layout, with a **linear cell index** and **sequential loops** for cache-friendly access.

---

## 2. Map

### 2.1 Structure and Generation

- The world is a **64 × 64 top-view cell grid**.
- Generation is **deterministic from a seed**, so the same seed always produces the same map.
- Territory is driven by a **2D noise heightmap**.
- Terrain types are **forest, meadow, water, swamp, hills, and desert**.

### 2.2 Visibility

- **Forest** reduces the party's observation radius.
- **Hills** increase it.


---

## 3. Movement and Time

### 3.1 Movement

The party is a **single unit**. The player **clicks any cell to jump there directly**. Pathfinding is not part of the prototype.

### 3.2 Time Model

The game runs in **real time with a hybrid control**: the player can pause or run at a slow speed.

---

## 4. Investigation

### 4.1 Search Area

Investigation covers a **3 × 3 or 5 × 5 area** around the party.

### 4.2 Progress

Each cell fills toward **100%**. The rate depends on the **cell type** and on the **continuous Euclidean distance** from the party's exact position to the cell centre.

### 4.3 Distance Multiplier

- The multiplier is **capped at 1**.
- Cells inside the **observation centre** (3 × 3 or 5 × 5) receive **1**.
- Distant cells receive a **smooth falloff** below 1.
- **Camping on hills** raises the multiplier toward 1, but never above it.

The distance function uses smooth float interpolation to cell centres, not grid steps.

### 4.4 View Mode

A view mode colours each cell by its **investigation percentage**.

---

## 5. Sites

### 5.1 Visibility Levels

Sites are either **immediately visible**, **unknown but interesting**, or **hidden** and requiring investigation.

### 5.2 Chance-Based Sites

- Some sites exist only for a **limited time**.
- **Presence** and **discovery** are separate probabilities.
- **Investigation increases the discovery chance.**
- An indicator shows when discovery is possible.

### 5.3 Site Types

| Type | Purpose | Payout behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| One-time resource collection | Gather a resource once | Paid once, added to inventory |
| Item collection | Gather an item once | Paid once |
| Party experience | Improve a skill or attribute | Granted to the party |
| Tasks and quests | Solve a problem for a reward or escape | Reward on completion |
| Persistent deposit | Extract a finite resource over time | Yields while stock remains, then depletes |

Tasks and items stay **abstract** so the setting can be fantasy, steampunk, social realism, or another theme.


# Phase 2 design document. It covers party structure, skills, competition, economy, and victory, and it builds on the Phase 1 map and investigation rules.

---

## 1. Party Structure

The party is a single travelling unit with **shared values** and **individual members**. Each member contributes base traits and skills, and the party's effective capability comes from combining them.

### 1.1 Shared Party Values

| Value | Meaning | Affected by |
|---|---|---|
| Condition | How well the party can keep travelling and working | Members' physical condition, terrain, rest, and supplies |
| Morale | Willingness to continue, especially after setbacks | Successes, failures, rest, and pay |
| Funds and supplies | What the party can attempt and how long it can stay out | Hiring costs, spending, and resource use |

### 1.2 Hiring and Dismissal

- Hiring adds a member's base traits and skills to the party.
- Each member has an ongoing cost.
- Dismissing a member reduces ongoing cost but removes their skills, which may leave a gap in the party's capabilities.

---

## 2. Members

### 2.1 Base Characteristics

Each member has base characteristics set at hiring. These are separate from trained skills.

| Characteristic | What it controls |
|---|---|
| Learning ability | Rate of skill gain from training and experience |
| Physical condition | Baseline endurance and strength, feeding party condition |
| Recovery rate | How quickly condition returns after rest |
| Growth potential | How far physical condition can improve with training |

Learning ability can change over time, but this is a **low-priority deferred task** and is not part of the current design.

### 2.2 Skills

Skills are merged into **six domains** to keep the party readable.

| Skill | Combines | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Navigation | Navigation | Efficient travel, fewer delays, route knowledge |
| Fieldcraft | Endurance, Field making | Shelter, routes, practical management of local conditions |
| Observation | Survey | Noticing sites, resources, and time-limited discoveries |
| Knowledge | Natural science, Record-keeping | Identifying materials and recording findings |
| Communication | Languages, Ethnography | Building trust, gaining access and information |
| Logistics | Logistics (organising supply and processing) | Reduced supply use and better resource processing |

Skills are raised by use and training. Each member's learning ability affects how quickly they improve.

### 2.3 Emergent Party Skills

When combined domain levels cross a threshold, the party unlocks a new capability. For example:

- Observation with Navigation unlocks **route charting**, which reveals cells beyond the normal observation radius.
- Knowledge with Logistics unlocks **field assay**, which estimates deposit quality before mining.
- Communication with Fieldcraft unlocks **local trust**, which opens certain site tasks.

---

## 3. Competition

### 3.1 Core Principle

There is **no fighting or wargaming**. Rivals compete through discovery, claims, extraction, and influence.

### 3.2 Discovery Race, Claims and Finite Sites

- **Priority claims:** the first party to fully record a site owns its primary value.
- **Finite sites:** deposits deplete as they are worked, so rivals compete for them.
- **Industrial efficiency:** population in cell, availability of resources (material, food, oxygen), special buildings (knowledge) nearbly + in whole party's controlled area determines how fast it can build mine, extract, and process.

### 3.3 Influence

- Influence is a continuous field in cells, using the same smooth falloff from sources.
- Sources include special claimed sites, camps, willages, some buildings, discoveries, etc.
- The party with higher influence can controll cell: investigate, build, think about it as cell population has attention to this party.
- Influence decays where a party stops maintaining its sources.

### 3.4 Deferred

Access, contracts, and reputation are **deferred** and are not part of this design.

Priority: **low**. Status: **needs review** — the draft has no details for these
systems, and they are unlikely to be interesting unless they are thought through
well before any implementation.

---

## 4. Economy

### 4.1 Stock-and-Flow Model

The economy is a set of stocks and flows. Resources, products, and recipes are **defined as data**, so new materials and industries can be added without redesigning the system.

Each deposit has:

- a **finite quantity**
- an **extraction rate**
- an **output** of resource
- a **disturbed area** left behind (see Section 5)

### 4.2 Layers

- **Resources:** easy, concentrated deposits are extracted first. Later, recovery of dispersed material needs investigation and processing knowledge.
- **Products:** produced from resources using energy and inputs.
- **Biosphere:** oxygen and carbon stocks. Forests, meadows, and swamps produce oxygen and consume carbon. Fuel and industry consume oxygen and add carbon. Surplus oxygen over fuel use is limited.

### 4.3 Conservation Ledger

Every transformation conserves mass and energy. Each process consumes inputs and produces outputs and dissipated heat, and the totals balance.

| Process | Inputs | Outputs | Conservation note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mining | Deposit stock, energy | Ore, disturbed material in the cell | Ore plus left-behind material equals mass removed |
| Fuel and industry | Fuel, oxygen, energy | Energy, carbon, heat, products | Mass balance holds through combustion |
| Photosynthesis | Carbon, sunlight | Oxygen, biomass | Carbon fixed into biomass; energy partly dissipated |
| Recycling | Disturbed material, energy | Recovered material | Recovered mass plus residue equals input; recycling costs energy |
| Natural breakdown | Disturbed material, biosphere activity | Slow return to the cycle | Limited by available energy |

Each tick, total mass and energy on the map should equal the previous total plus external input (sunlight) minus dissipated heat. This works as a built-in test for bugs.

## 5. Land Condition

### 5.1 Disturbed Area Instead of Waste

Waste is **not a material type**. Each cell tracks a **disturbed fraction** `d`, from 0 (untouched) to 1 (fully disturbed). Mining and factory deposition add to `d` by the area they cover.

### 5.2 Effects

- **Productivity** = `base × (1 − d)`
- **Farming** is allowed only while `d` is below a threshold, for example 0.2.
- **Growth** of forests, meadows, and swamps follows the same productivity factor.
- **Appearance** blends the normal colour toward a degraded colour by `d`.

### 5.3 Recovery

- **Natural recovery** is slow, depends on terrain, and slows as disturbance grows: speed scales with `(1 − d)`, so fully disturbed land (`d = 1`) does not recover naturally, while lightly disturbed land recovers near the terrain rate but never instantly — it cannot outrun disturbance left by deposits and factories.
- **Active restoration** (planting, soil work, water management) reduces `d` faster. Each action costs energy and resources and is recorded in the ledger.
- Remote repair is possible in some cases, provided the party pays the energy and transport cost.

## 7. Win and Survival

### 7.1 Survival

A party fails if its members are lost to endurance and hardship, if its oxygen and fuel balance collapses in a region, or if it runs out of supplies with no way to recover. Survival is a precondition for winning.

### 7.2 Victory

- There is **no time limit**. The **player decides when to declare victory**.
- A declaration is valid when the player holds the **largest territory share** and the **economy is stable**: it does not depend on depleting resources, produces no unmanaged disturbance, and runs an energy balance of zero or better.
- When the player declares, the game checks these conditions. If one fails, the player receives feedback on which one is missing.

## 8. Open Questions

- **Victory detail:** the precise meaning of "unmanaged disturbance" and how the energy balance is sourced.
- **Recovery rates:** terrain rates set; recovery scales with `(1 − d)` — fully disturbed land does not recover naturally. Decided, see section 5.3.
- **Rival damage persistence:** whether a rival's damage stays after they leave, or recovers on its own.
- **Learning ability changes:** deferred, low priority.
- **Deferred systems:** access, contracts, and reputation.
